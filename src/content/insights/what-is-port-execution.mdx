Port execution is the coordination of the shore-side activities required to move cargo from a supplier through customs, storage and transportation to a vessel. It sits between "the cargo exists" and "the cargo is onboard" — and it's where most of the operational risk in a vessel delivery actually lives.

## Why it's a distinct discipline

A vessel call is a fixed point in time. Everything feeding into it — supplier dispatch, documentation, customs clearance, warehousing, transportation — has to resolve before that window closes. Port execution is the practice of coordinating those dependencies as one operation rather than a series of handoffs between unrelated providers.

## What it typically involves

- Supplier coordination and cargo readiness checks
- Documentation review and customs liaison
- Storage and, where useful, consolidation of multiple shipments
- Transportation scheduled around the vessel's delivery window
- Final delivery to the vessel and confirmation
